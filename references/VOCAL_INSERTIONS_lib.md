# Vocal insertions: function, source, content and processing

A selection library, not mandatory ornamentation or official tag grammar. Use [VOCAL_lib.md](../references/VOCAL_lib.md), [MAIN_THEME_lib.md](../references/MAIN_THEME_lib.md) and [SUNO_TAGS_lib.md](../references/SUNO_TAGS_lib.md) as needed. All cues/descriptions are English; performed words are original in the requested language.

## Selection card and notation

Purpose → stable voice/ensemble → words or nonverbal sound → entrance/exit → pattern/delivery → theme relation → processing → risk/simpler alternative. A single breath needs less detail than a recurring part. Do not add an insertion merely to increase length. Source, delivery, arrangement role and FX are distinct. Groups need not be male; choirs need not be church/classical.

M below means estimated medium risk for a short descriptive cue; exact notes, microtiming, sample cuts, routing or FX chains may be H. Basis: DESIGN/analogy, not measured audio or confirmed syntax. A catalog noun does not establish a new bracketed control tag. Needed high/unknown syntax follows [SUNO_TAGS_lib.md](SUNO_TAGS_lib.md): maximum Lyrics grants permission, including delegated selection, unless an explicit safer restriction applies. Free music mode alone is not that permission.

Use section → musical cues → explicit voice cue → words. A brief combined cue such as [Female Vocal: brief whispered response] is a desired delivery. Duet must match Styles. Wordless support may use [Backing Vocals: soft harmony]; support with words needs its own assigned block and explicit lead return. Keep words outside [Vocal Hook: quoted words]; avoid plain role labels and technical sample/MIDI/timecode commands.

## 1. Melodic and structural elements

| Element | Function / distinction | Concise English description | Simplification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vocal Hook | Memorable return, inside chorus or independent | short recurring vocal hook, clear rhythmic identity | Own short phrase on separate lines |
| Vocal Line / Phrase | Complete melodic/semantic phrase, lead/answer/original sample-like part | brief melodic vocal phrase between lead lines | One assigned performed line |
| Vocal Melisma | Several notes on one syllable; not necessarily fast | restrained melisma on the final syllable | Held syllable |
| Vocal Runs | Quick note sequence, often melismatic; not every phrase | brief vocal run at the phrase ending | Simple melodic ornament; exact speed/notes H |
| Vocal Riff | Repeating vocal pattern with riff function, possibly the main motif | repeating rhythmic vocal riff with a rising contour | Short original phrase/syllable pattern |

Hook is a function, line a phrase, riff a repeating figure. One element can fulfill several functions without becoming three competing main themes.

## 2. Processed forms

| Element | Function / distinction | Description | Simplification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vocal Chops | Cut/reordered voice fragments forming rhythm/new melody; MIDI keyboard optional | short rhythmic vocal chops following the main motif | Short vocal riff; exact slicing H |
| Vocal Textures / Pads | Sustained/processed atmosphere or harmonic support, not necessarily choir | soft wordless vocal pad behind the lead | Quiet sustained vocalise; exact granular chain H |
| Glitch Vocal | Intentional stutters, microrepeats/cuts or digital disruption | brief stuttered vocal accents before the transition | Short rhythmic accents; exact milliseconds H |
| Pitched Vocal | Shifted pitch, not automatically a track key change | lightly pitched vocal doubles, retaining the original source voice | Intentional octave doubling |
| Formant-shifted Vocal | Resonance/timbre change, potentially independent of pitch | darker formant-shifted vocal texture, unchanged melodic contour | Darker/lighter color |

Pitch and formants are not synonyms. Chipmunk/monster effects may combine processing; a high voice is not automatically formant-shifted. Request character without claiming an actual plugin/DAW operation or exact reference replication.

## 3. Background and dynamic elements

| Element | Function | Description | Simplification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ad-libs | Own extra replies/exclamations/end repeats, not necessarily background | sparse ad-lib responses in the gaps between lead phrases | One assigned response, not a fixed shout pack |
| Chants / Group Chants | Rhythmic chanting by one voice/group | short group chants marking the rhythmic accents | Short phrase by the chosen cast |
| Vocal Shouts | Brief strong accent; energetic need not mean aggressive | brief shouted vocal accent at the section entrance | Expressive attack |
| Screams / Shrieks | Extreme/sharp expression with intentionally chosen timbre | one brief harsh vocal scream before the drop | Strong shout; exact extreme processing H |
| Vocal Percussion / Beatbox | Vocal drum imitation or independent rhythm | dry vocal percussion reinforcing the offbeat rhythm | Short rhythm accent; exact phoneme grid H |

Avoid accidental masking of meaningful lead words. Chants may contain words; percussion may be non-narrative. Rhyme function follows the actual text.

## 4. Introductory and identity elements

| Element | Function | Description | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Producer Tag | User's original signature, only if requested | brief original spoken signature in the intro | Own words, never a third-party recognizable signature |
| Vocal Intro / Outro | Original speech, whisper or singing at boundaries | intimate spoken vocal intro, returning softly in the outro | Assign source under Intro/Outro |
| Spoken Word | Speech/declamation without pronounced sung melody, rhythmic or free | calm spoken-word passage with natural pauses | Own short phrase |

Spelling: Spoken Word. Do not source words from film/interview quotes, reference lyrics or others' sonic signatures. Translate references into musical properties. Resolve any user signature that conflicts with banned reference identity rather than bypassing chunk cleaning.

## 5. Breathing and nonverbal expression

| Element | Function / distinction | Description | Simplification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breaths | Chosen singer's inhalation/exhalation | subtle audible breath before the vocal entrance | Natural close delivery; exact isolated one-shot H |
| Sighs | Longer expressive exhalation: relief/fatigue/regret by context | one restrained sigh after the final phrase | Soft phrase ending |
| Whispers | Quiet articulation, not silence or automatic ASMR | close whispered delivery with clear words | Quiet close voice; exact stereo placement H |
| Grunts / Groans | Short guttural or longer low sounds; meaning is contextual | brief low vocal grunt as a rhythmic accent | Low brief accent |
| Laughs / Giggles | Chosen source's laughter with intentional emotion | one brief soft laugh between phrases | Playful accent |
| Cries / Sobs | Separate sobs or crying delivery; distinguish them | restrained sobbing texture in the bridge | Restrained broken delivery |
| Clicks / Clucks | Mouth/tongue micropercussion, not necessarily teeth sounds | sparse tongue clicks reinforcing the groove | Short percussion accent |
| Humming | Closed-mouth wordless melody, possibly the main contour | soft humming of the main motif | Soft wordless vocalise |

Repeated user categories for breaths/whispers/grunts/shouts are consolidated here. Do not automatically turn effects into ha/ah/uh syllables: those become performed content only by choice. Whispered words remain words. Wordless voice is not voiceless instrumental music.

## 6. Collective and multiple voices

| Element | Function / distinction | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Vocal Choir | Ensemble color: chamber, church, modern or other | small mixed choir supporting the final refrain |
| Gang Vocals | Raw shared singing/shouts; not necessarily male | rough mixed group vocals on the refrain |
| Harmonies | Additional compatible melodic pitches, intervals only when useful | soft backing harmonies around the lead melody |
| Backing Vocals | Supporting role: harmony, answer, doubling, etc. | female backing responses only at phrase endings |
| Unison Chants | Shared phrase/pattern at the same pitches; not necessarily one note | short unison group chant, shared rhythmic phrasing |

General descriptions M; exact voice-leading/routing may be H. Unison shares melody/pitch; octave doubling shifts by octave; harmony uses other compatible lines. Ensemble/role terms are not rhyme schemes or automatic shared leads. Same-gender parts need stable distinctions, not invented official Singer A tags.

## Placement, theme and views

Decide whether the track is led by sung melody, instrumental motif, riff/chops or another core. Each insertion repeats/answers/contrasts it or marks a transition; incidental sounds do not become another main theme automatically.

Styles states source, general character and recurring role. Lyrics shows relevant entrances/withdrawals/changes. Replace excessive FX chains with audible results. Planned overlap is possible, but words need intentional space.

Strict musical mode favors brief-compatible conventions; free mode allows unusual purposeful choices. Musical and lyric modes remain independent. For an instrumental with explicitly requested wordless chops, record both absence of words and presence of the selected voice; add no further singing/speech.

Minimal text views retain structure, English ownership/delivery and significant vocal insertions beside performed lines, dropping detailed FX/instrument cues. Explicit plain views keep performed words/intentional syllables but omit nonverbal descriptions; never turn laugh into a sung word. If no words exist, say so; the full vocal/music scenario is available as full Lyrics. Imported quoted words require review, not silent loss.
