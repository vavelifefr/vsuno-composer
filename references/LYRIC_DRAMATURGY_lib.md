# Lyrical premise, progression and song-specific vocabulary

Use with LYRICS_flow for new lyrics, subject changes and singable translation. These are composition decisions, not extra user questions or mandatory output fields.

Establish central meaning, narrator/addressee when relevant, situation/atmosphere, emotional movement and protected content. A hook expresses that premise or a deliberate musical function. Do not replace the author's meaning to find easier rhyme.

Story: beginning and outcome. Atmosphere: state, concrete image/action and recurring phrase function. Minimal rave: an original short hook can be sufficient; no forced story/addressee/three verses. Instrumentals without words do not need this library.

Narrator and singer differ: male/female delivery does not determine character gender, age or perspective. A voice change does not automatically change narrator. Deliberate dialogue assigns perspectives and pronouns.

| Approach | Possible movement | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Story | Situation, action, consequence, decision | Later verse adds an event |
| Confession/address | Detail, spoken feeling, doubt/response, changed stance | Addressee/pronouns remain clear |
| Dialogue | First view, answer, divergence/convergence | Assigned character answers the preceding thought |
| Observation | Moment, meaning, new detail/perspective | Image serves the thought |
| Atmosphere | Image, intensification/thinning, return | Repetition creates state rather than random words |
| Minimal hook/chant | Short phrase, rhythmic return/response | Concise accent function |
| Through-composed | Developing stanzas | No compulsory Chorus; refrain is purposeful |

These are options, not genre laws. Preserve an existing text's logic unless revision asks otherwise.

Give each necessary section a contribution to the premise. Each line may act, observe, answer, state or deliberately repeat; it need not introduce a new fact. Check causality, tense/place/pronouns and believable speech. A generic line needs a stronger connection to this speaker/brief. A final chorus variation changes meaning while preserving protected hook and compatible phrasing/rhyme.

Choose a temporary field of actions, objects/places, sensory details, idioms and metaphor relationships appropriate to narrator, language, era and text mode. Do not use every candidate or add slang automatically. No permanent pretty-word list, dance-night vocabulary or cross-song repetition log. User bans live in the current brief/state; examples are not song material.

Local line edits preserve function and linked rhyme. A new lyric subject preserves approved music/cast/form. Translation preserves relationships and emotional motion. The full selected chunk remains authoritative.

Basis: [Berklee lyrics](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/how-to-write-song-lyrics/), [Berklee prosody](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/prosody-in-music-and-songwriting/). The workflow is skill design, not a guaranteed artistic result.
