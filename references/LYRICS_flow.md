# Deliberate lyric writing

Use [LYRICS_RHYME_lib.md](../references/LYRICS_RHYME_lib.md), [LYRIC_DRAMATURGY_lib.md](../references/LYRIC_DRAMATURGY_lib.md), [PROSODY_lib.md](../references/PROSODY_lib.md) and relevant vocal resources. Preserve source words/locks during revision.

1. Establish a short premise: who speaks, to whom, what happens/changes, emotional stance and a few concrete details. Performer is not narrator. Each verse adds something; a chorus articulates the central claim.
2. Gather original sensory/action material before forcing rhyme. Avoid default genre vocabulary and adjective piles. No unsolicited global word-ban log; honor current user bans.
3. Assign section meaning, voices and performed language separately. Use the English-language or Russian-language generation profile in LYRICS_RHYME_lib for each block, including inserts/backing; in a mixed song do not apply one profile globally. Distinguish story progress, chorus return, bridge turn, hook and instrumental space. Do not force a verse/chorus form on every style.
4. Design the hook and phrase shape: length, stress, delivery, repeats and meaningful final variation. Keep it memorable without copying reference words.
5. Build rhyme families from semantic anchors, not a random ending list. Choose scheme, sound precision, internal links and delayed returns by section. Differentiate verse density from chorus function, without making every hook short or every rap line long.
6. Draft performed words in natural syntax and singable groups. Respect supplied melody if available; otherwise do not claim exact note/duration alignment. Preserve natural pronunciation.
7. Check selected sound pairs and phrases locally. Compare actual stressed vowels, tails and delivery; distinguish exact, slant, assonant and repeated words. Dictionaries can assist, not certify the whole song.
8. Check draft meaning: concrete action, credible voice, progress, line necessity and natural language. Repair the semantic choice, not just its final synonym. Never keep a senseless line for rhyme.
9. Fit words into the approved skeleton and role map. Important phrase -> semantic stress -> delivery/pause -> musical response. Assign each multi-voice block after section changes. Insertions need source, purpose, original words/nonverbal content, placement and motif relationship. Processing preserves ownership. Check that Exclude permits needed rap/choir/blend components.
10. Perform one final review under [LYRIC_REVIEW_lib.md](../references/LYRIC_REVIEW_lib.md): meaning/language, sound, performance/music. Repair only affected passages and dependencies; do not restart composition or repeat independent full audits. Recheck changed dependencies after compression.

This is a working procedure, not a demand to reveal hidden reasoning. Helper exit0 cannot certify rhyme, originality, complex ownership or Suno performance.

Foundations: [Berklee: How to Write Song Lyrics](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/how-to-write-song-lyrics/) and [Elements of Lyrics](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/lyric-elements-the-great-juggling-act/). The LLM sequence and Suno integration are skill design. Do not copy their artistic examples into user songs.
