# Prosody, stress, phrasing and breathing

Prosody aligns words and delivery with musical and semantic purpose. Poetic meter, musical time signature, syllable count, note rhythm and phrase length are distinct. Equal printed length does not establish singability. A 4/4 bar does not demand four words; 6/8 does not demand six lines.

## Phrase design

Choose useful phrase length, stress pattern, strong words, ending and pauses for each section. Similar melodic returns need compatible stress/ending positions, not necessarily identical syllable counts. Verse can carry detail; chorus may use stronger/longer anchors; rap density follows subdivision and delivery; minimal chants need only their function.

Without supplied melody, use a plausible relative phrase shape and state that exact syllable-note alignment is unverified. With melody/locked words, fit phrasing first; rewriting needs authorized adaptation.

## Breathing and punctuation

Sources L06/L07 support considering vocal phrase length and breath space, not a universal word ceiling or Suno punctuation command. A 7-10-word sentence can work; a shorter dense line can fail. Tempo, subdivision, consonants, melisma, register and pauses affect workload.

Break long dense thoughts into grammatical/sense groups. Line breaks and appropriate commas, semicolons, dashes or sentence boundaries can clarify phrasing, not guarantee an inhalation. Do not split a word, corrupt grammar or hide a stress error with punctuation. Dense rap may span bars and retain enjambment; leave phrase space rather than accelerating every section.

Read the line naturally, identify semantic/stressed words, then compare intended pulse and rhyme partner. Heavy line -> remove optional material or rebuild syntax -> recheck stress/ending/meaning/stanza. A short line can hold a vowel or leave silence; avoid filler. Repair the troublesome phrase/response first, not whole tempo or successful neighboring lines.

Melisma, holding a syllable and repeating words are different. Keep normal spelling; do not hyphenate/capitalize every syllable. A breath instruction is a short English cue with risk under VOCAL_INSERTIONS, never a literal breath word inserted into lyrics.

## Russian and English

Use the two language profiles in [LYRICS_RHYME_lib.md](../references/LYRICS_RHYME_lib.md). Russian blocks need actual word-form stress, ending-tail space, grammar and meaningful vowels including ё. English blocks need pronunciation/dialect, lexical versus sentence emphasis and natural contractions/idiom. Never infer phrase capacity from word count or impose a universal English-to-Russian length ratio. A mixed song selects a profile for each block, independently of voice; connect cadence/accents without mechanically copying syllable counts.

## Vocal melody and semantic emphasis

Range is the span between extremes; tessitura is the region used most often. Male/female labels do not establish a singer's range. Without actual performer data, choose relative register/character rather than inventing physiological limits.

Coordinate working register, stepwise movement versus leaps, melodic peak, held vowel, runs/melisma and a second singer's response. A response need not duplicate the melody. On a held word, consonants retain intelligibility and its vowel carries duration; no universal vowel is ideal for every high note/voice/language.

Choose the important word and its intended accent: entry, syncopation, leap, hold or following pause. Do not accidentally peak on a filler/function word, or assign every line all ornaments. Intentional function-word emphasis is valid when meaningful. More words are not necessarily more expressive.

Styles gives global register/delivery and motif relationships; Lyrics places important accents, pauses and responses. These are DESIGN suggestions with estimated risk, not exact score control. Include them in the existing final review.
